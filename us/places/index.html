Where to live in the Algarve

There is no single “best Algarve”. Pick the trade-offs that fit your life, then test them in person: summer and winter feel different, and a beautiful view does not tell you how a Tuesday works.

Begin with your ordinary week

Write down how often you need an airport, hospital, school, supermarket, coworking space, beach, public transport or community. Decide how much driving you accept and whether you prefer a year-round town or a quieter seasonal setting. Use real addresses to test journeys.

Western Algarve

Lagos and nearby towns

Historic centre, varied neighbourhoods and coastal access, with tourism pressure and seasonal demand to consider. Compare town living with surrounding villages for transport and winter services.

Central coast

Portimão and Alvor

Larger service base and hospital access nearby; different urban, marina and resort patterns. Check traffic, building type, noise and the exact distance to daily needs.

Central Algarve

Loulé, Faro and Olhão

More year-round civic, transport and service options, including proximity to Faro airport. Compare inland town life, city neighbourhoods and coastal communities rather than treating the area as one place.

Eastern Algarve

Tavira and beyond

Distinctive town character and access to the eastern coast, with travel time to services and airport varying by exact location. Visit in winter and test your preferred routine.

Score each location against your actual needs

FactorWhat to test
HealthcareJourney time at different hours, local health centre registration and your chosen private providers.
TransportAirport routes, public transit, parking, road access and whether you can manage without a car.
SeasonalityOpen shops and services in winter; noise, traffic and short-term rental pressure in summer.
HousingActual condition, damp, heating/cooling, internet, running costs, legal status and maintenance.
CommunityLanguage access, interests, clubs, volunteering and whether the place suits your preferred social pace.
ResilienceWater, shade, fire exposure, drainage, insurance availability and access during disruptions.

A simple visit plan

Stay at least several nights in each shortlisted area. Shop, commute, walk after dark, visit outside the tourist centre, and ask residents what changes between seasons. If you may buy, use an independent local lawyer and technical inspector; a viewing tour is not due diligence.
