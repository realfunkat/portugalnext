Bringing a dog or cat from the US

For a dog or cat traveling with its owner from the US, the order of the microchip, rabies vaccination and paperwork can determine the earliest possible flight. Bring the itinerary to a USDA-accredited veterinarian before buying a nonrefundable ticket.

Work back from the first EU airport

Write down where you first enter the EU, the scheduled arrival time there, the final destination, and who is traveling with the pet. A connection through Lisbon from the US puts Lisbon first even if your home is in the Algarve. If you connect through another EU country, confirm the inspection arrangements there. A pet traveling separately, changing ownership or traveling as part of a larger group may need a different certificate.

What to do, and in what order

  1. Have the chip scanned. Ask the vet to read it and compare every digit with the vaccination record. USDA requires the working chip to have been in place before the rabies vaccination used for travel. Ask the vet to review the sequence if the only vaccine on file predates the chip.
  2. Count the vaccine waiting period. After a primary vaccination, allow at least 21 days, or the manufacturer’s longer immunity period where applicable. A lapse in vaccine coverage can change the date on which travel is allowed. Have the vet calculate the earliest entry date from the records.
  3. Arrange the certificate and endorsement. Use the current USDA Portugal instructions for your type of movement. For the usual accompanying-owner, noncommercial trip, USDA endorsement must fall within the ten-day window before EU arrival. Ask the practice how the endorsed document will reach you and what you must carry at inspection.
  4. Notify the Portuguese entry point. When arriving directly in Portugal from outside the EU, the owner or authorized person must give the designated travelers’ entry point written notice at least 48 hours before arrival. Use DGAV’s form and the entry point’s contact, save the response and ask where to present the animal after landing.

Get the airline’s acceptance for every leg

Give each operating carrier the pet’s weight, breed, carrier dimensions and travel date. Ask whether the pet travels in the cabin or hold, whether space is confirmed on each flight, and what happens during a connection. For a large dog, check the aircraft and crate limits before selecting a route. If the airline changes your flight, recheck inspection arrangements, notice and certificate dates too.

The first week in your Algarve home

Before arrival, get written confirmation that the accommodation accepts your pet, including its size. Arrange a vehicle that fits the carrier with your luggage. Walk the boundary of any garden, check gates and gaps, and locate a veterinary practice and an out-of-hours service. Take the US records to the first appointment and ask the vet to check the chip, arrange the applicable Portuguese SIAC registration and record a reachable local contact for you.
