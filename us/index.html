A considered starting point

Your move deserves more than a visa checklist. Use this guide to understand the decisions, sequence the work, and know which questions need a qualified professional before you commit.

A serious move needs a joined-up plan

Moving from the United States to Portugal touches immigration, tax, healthcare, housing, banking, work and family decisions. These topics interact. The order matters: a visa route can shape your timing, a property purchase does not grant residence, and a tax-residence date can affect more than one tax return.

Start with three decisions.
  1. What will be your basis to live in Portugal: own income, remote employment, local work, family or another eligible route?
  2. When might Portuguese tax residence begin, and what US filing obligations will continue?
  3. What kind of ordinary week do you want: town-centre life, a quieter village, access to services, countryside or coastal living?

The order we recommend

  1. Test the move before committing. Visit outside peak season, rent in more than one area, and record real travel times to healthcare, shops, airports and the people you expect to see.
  2. Map your immigration route. Match the route to your actual income and work arrangement, not just a visa label from a forum. Build the official document checklist early.
  3. Take a cross-border tax inventory. List citizenships, days in each country, income types, retirement accounts, entities, property and foreign accounts. Ask a US–Portugal adviser to model the move date.
  4. Secure healthcare continuity. Confirm coverage for the transition and any pre-existing treatment. Do not assume Medicare follows you abroad.
  5. Choose housing after due diligence. Check title, use permissions, building condition, running costs, access and the precise transaction costs before paying a deposit.
  6. Coordinate the move and arrival tasks. Keep copies of civil records, apostilles and translations; plan tax number, bank, residence appointment, healthcare registration and driving requirements.

What this guide can and cannot do

It gives you the framework, official entry points and questions to take to your own licensed professionals. It cannot assess your facts like your immigration lawyer, US tax preparer, Portuguese accountant or doctor.
