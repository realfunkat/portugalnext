Working remotely or in Portugal

“I can work from anywhere” is a business arrangement, not an immigration or payroll conclusion. Have both your employer/client arrangement and your residence route reviewed before you move.

Four different approvals may be involved

  • Immigration: does your residence status permit the activity you plan to carry out?
  • Employer and labour: has your employer approved Portugal as a work location, and can it lawfully employ/payroll you there?
  • Tax and social security: where will income tax withholding and contributions be due? Which system applies under the relevant rules?
  • Business presence: could your activity create registration, VAT, permanent-establishment or corporate obligations?

If you are employed by a US company

Obtain written approval describing where you may work, for how long, who handles payroll, benefits, insurance, data security and expenses. Ask whether the employer has a Portuguese entity or employer-of-record arrangement and whether the proposed arrangement is compatible with your visa. A US payroll setup alone does not answer Portuguese compliance questions.

If you are self-employed or run a business

Confirm which visa category fits and what evidence is needed. Ask a Portuguese accountant about activity registration, invoices, VAT, social-security contributions and recordkeeping. A US LLC or corporation does not automatically map neatly onto Portuguese categories. Ask US counsel/accounting support about entity classification and any continuing filings.

If you want local employment

Start with the legal right to work, the contract and the employer’s recruitment/onboarding obligations. Compare salary after tax and mandatory contributions, not just gross salary. Confirm probation, notice, paid leave, insurance and pension arrangements in the actual contract.

Questions to settle in writing

  • Which residence route authorises this specific activity?
  • Who is the legal employer/client, and where is the work physically performed?
  • Who withholds income tax and pays social-security contributions?
  • What happens to US health, disability, retirement and equity benefits?
  • Could my work create a taxable business presence or registration obligation?
  • What records should I keep for travel days, invoices and expenses?
Ewa’s team can help coordinate practical introductions and local setup. Employment, tax and immigration conclusions should come from the appropriately qualified professionals reviewing your documents.

The email to send before you book a move

Give HR a specific proposal: “I would like to work from Faro from September 1. I will keep managing the same US accounts and expect to travel to the US four times a year. Which entity would employ me, who will run payroll, and which benefits will apply while I am in Portugal?” Include whether you negotiate or sign customer contracts and whether the move is temporary or open-ended. Those details give payroll and legal teams something concrete to assess.

The US–Portugal social-security agreement has rules for certain temporary assignments. Where an exemption applies, a certificate of coverage documents which system covers the worker. Ask payroll who will check eligibility, request the certificate and track its end date. A personal request to relocate may have different facts from an employer posting someone abroad. Walk through the first freelance invoice

Bring a draft contract and a sample invoice to a Portuguese accountant before accepting the first payment. Identify the customer’s country, whether the customer is a business or an individual, the service, where you will perform it, and the currency. Have the accountant mark up that specific invoice with the appropriate registration, invoicing and VAT treatment and explain what goes in your records. If a US company will invoice instead, include its ownership and how you plan to take money out.
