Choosing a school in the Algarve

If you are moving with children, start the school search while your choice of town is still open. A confirmed place, a manageable school run and the right teaching language can narrow the housing search considerably.

Choose a curriculum as well as a language

A Portuguese public school gives a child a route into the local school community and a Portuguese-language education. Ask about Português Língua Não Materna (PLNM), support for pupils learning Portuguese. Find out who assesses the child, what support is available in their year group, and how parents receive updates during the first term.

Private and international schools vary by campus. For example, Nobel Algarve in Lagoa publishes separate documents for its Portuguese national section and its international section following the English curriculum. An English-language classroom can therefore lead to British qualifications. If your teenager expects to return to a US high school, ask the US counselor which subjects and credits will transfer before choosing courses here.

Prepare the records while the US school can help

  1. Ask the receiving school about placement. Give your child’s date of birth, last completed grade, current subjects, languages and proposed arrival month. Ask how it handles midyear arrivals.
  2. Collect the underlying records. Request transcripts, recent report cards, grading scale and a contact for the registrar. For a high-school student, keep course descriptions and work in progress. Ask the receiving school which records need translation or authentication before paying for either.
  3. Check recognition of previous studies. The Portuguese government provides an equivalence process for foreign primary and secondary education. Ask the school which family member should apply, what evidence is needed, and what can be done while a decision is pending.
  4. Discuss learning support directly. Bring an existing US IEP or 504 plan if relevant. Ask who can provide each accommodation, in which language and at what cost. Get the proposed arrangements in writing.

Apply from a real address

For public enrollment, start with the government’s migrant education guide and its link to the Portal das Matrículas. Ask the local school group (agrupamento de escolas) which office serves the address you are considering and how to apply if you lack Portuguese online credentials. Confirm the current enrollment window, documents and placement process before signing a lease on the assumption that a specific school has space.

Compare a full year and a full school day

For a fee-paying school, ask for tuition for your child’s year group, registration or re-enrollment fees, meals, transport, uniforms, devices, exams and after-school care. Nobel Lagoa, for example, publishes separate fee and bus schedules. Ask what you forfeit if your visa is delayed. For either school route, visit at dismissal time and find out how children get home after clubs, when the normal bus may have left.

A family deciding between a home in Lagos and a school in Lagoa can try both journeys on a regular school day. Include the return trip for the parent who drives. Four half-hour legs would take two hours out of a working day. A useful question for your child after the visit is “Who would you go to if you got lost or didn’t understand the homework?”
