US–Portugal tax planning

A move can create filing duties in both countries, while citizenship, source of income, account ownership and residence dates each matter. Build an inventory and use advisers who routinely coordinate US and Portuguese returns.

Keep three questions separate

  1. Where are you tax resident? Portugal’s rules can consider the number of days and whether you maintain a home in circumstances indicating habitual residence. A US citizen may remain within the US tax system even after moving.
  2. Which country taxes each item? Wages, self-employment, pensions, Social Security, dividends, interest, rental income, capital gains, trusts and business interests can be treated differently.
  3. Which forms and disclosures apply? A tax payment calculation does not replace information reporting. Foreign financial accounts and specified foreign assets have separate reporting tests and thresholds.
Do this before you move: prepare a list of accounts and assets with maximum balances, account holders, signature authority, entity ownership, income source and expected transaction dates. Give the same inventory to your US and Portuguese advisers.

US obligations do not simply stop at the border

US citizens generally continue filing US federal income tax returns on worldwide income when filing thresholds and other rules require it. Exclusions, foreign tax credits, treaty provisions and totalisation rules may affect the calculation, but they are not interchangeable. The foreign earned income exclusion, for example, does not automatically eliminate self-employment tax or reporting obligations.

Reporting foreign accounts

FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) can apply when the aggregate value of foreign financial accounts exceeds $10,000 at any time during the calendar year. Form 8938 is a separate IRS disclosure with different thresholds, definitions and filing rules. A Portuguese account may need to be reviewed for more than one reporting regime. Ask about pensions, joint accounts, signing authority, companies and trusts too.

Portugal tax residence and the move date

Portuguese tax residence may arise under day-count or habitual-home criteria. The actual dates you arrive, rent or buy a home, bring family, start work and establish ordinary life can be relevant. Do not choose a date purely to fit a forum post or a calendar-year assumption.

Build an adviser brief

Give your advisersAsk them to answer
Citizenship, green-card history, US state ties, family members and move datesWhen does Portuguese residence begin, and what US state/residence issues remain?
All income streams, pensions, IRAs/401(k)s, equity compensation and expected salesHow is each item sourced and taxed in both countries? Which relief is available?
Foreign bank, brokerage, pension, company and trust accounts; peak valuesWhich FBAR, Form 8938, tax-return or other disclosures apply?
US real estate, Portuguese property, business interests and planned transfersWould a sale, gift, restructuring or distribution before/after moving change the result?
Do not restructure casually. Cross-border treatment of US retirement accounts, trusts, LLCs, investment funds and insurance products can be materially different. Get written, coordinated advice before changing ownership or making a large distribution.

A move-year example

Imagine a couple who spend April and May scouting, return to the US, then take possession of an Algarve home in September. One partner starts working there in October. Give the advisers the actual nights in each country, the lease, the move-in date and the first working day. Portuguese tax residence can arise from spending more than 183 days in a relevant 12-month period, or from maintaining a home in circumstances indicating an intention to occupy it as a habitual residence. A September arrival alone does not settle the question.

If the couple is also considering an IRA distribution or a US house sale, ask both advisers to compare the same two proposed transaction dates. For each date, request the expected tax in each country, available relief, cash needed for payments and records to retain. List acquisition cost and improvements as well as the sale price for the house. A US estimate alone leaves half the calculation open.

Why the highest balance matters

Suppose one US citizen holds two reportable Portuguese accounts worth $6,000 and $5,000 on the same day, in dollar equivalents. Together they reach $11,000, above the $10,000 FBAR threshold, even if both are nearly empty by December. Keep statements for accounts opened or closed during the year, including the one used briefly for a house deposit. Ask the preparer to assess Form 8938 separately using your filing status and circumstances. IRS foreign-account reporting guidance
