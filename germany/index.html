    The tax question

    Property ownership and tax residence are different decisions.

    Portugal's tax authority says tax residence can arise after more than 183 days in a relevant 12-month period, or where a home is maintained in circumstances showing an intention to occupy it as a habitual residence. Residents are generally taxed on worldwide income. Your treaty position, pension, investments and departure-country rules need individual review.

    Read the Portuguese Tax Authority's residence rules

    The former NHR regime closed to most new arrivals in 2024. IFICI is narrower and focused on eligible professional activity; retirees should not assume they qualify. Official tax-regime update

    A home purchase is one part of the move

    A home purchase is separate from your residence status, tax residence and due diligence. Check property, planning and financing questions with qualified professionals before you commit.

    Portuguese government services

    Healthcare is a plan, not an assumption

    Before choosing a town, check how you will access primary care, private hospitals, prescriptions and emergency services from that specific address. Ask an adviser about your registration route, insurance needs and any pension-linked healthcare entitlement.

    Safety needs local context

    Portugal's 2025 security report recorded a 1.6% fall in reported violent and serious crime and a 3.1% rise in total recorded crime. These national year-on-year figures provide context, not a neighbourhood comparison or a guarantee of safety.

    Price the real move

    Start with the housing numbers—not the brochure.

    Portugal can be less expensive than some high-cost cities, but the Algarve is one of the country's priciest housing regions. Compare a specific town and property type with your current home; do not rely on national averages.

    Budget beyond the purchase price

    • Acquisition taxes and stamp duty, whose amount depends on price, use and buyer circumstances.
    • Independent lawyer, land registry and notarial costs; technical inspection and any specialist reports.
    • Renovation, insurance, furnishing, utilities, pool/garden maintenance and the annual IMI property tax.
    • Currency transfer, financing and ongoing costs at home and in Portugal while you change tax residence.

    A buyer's questions before an offer

    • Does the registered use match the property and your intended full-time or holiday use?
    • Are title, boundaries, licences and any extensions or renovations in order? Ask your lawyer to verify.
    • What are the water, sewage, internet, access, noise, damp, orientation and maintenance realities?
    • Get independent legal and technical advice. An agent's listing or a local scout's observations are not due diligence.
    Test the everyday life

    Make your first visit answer real questions.

    A week of viewings can leave you with a folder of listings and little sense of how a place works. Use a few days to test the routine you expect to live.

    A four-day reconnaissance plan

    Adapt this to your needs. A winter visit can answer questions that a summer holiday cannot.

    Day 1Drive the normal route from airport to likely areas. Check access, parking and what errands require a car.
    Day 2Spend a weekday morning in the town centre. Visit a supermarket, pharmacy, market and health centre.
    Day 3Walk the streets around any serious property candidate. Return at dusk; note noise, lighting, privacy and neighbours.
    Day 4Run your likely weekly routine: commute, golf or beach, medical appointment, airport journey and a quiet evening.
